# Design log

One entry per design round in Milestone 4 (D-004). Record what Scott saw, what he asked to change, and what changed. The design is settled only when Scott says so.

## Round 0: two directions
Date: 2026-10-14
What Scott saw: two static directions for the Threshold and the Room at the local-only routes /design-review/a and /design-review/b, on real catalog thumbnails and the approved room names. Both start at the center mood (0, 0). The Room ordering on these pages is a stand-in so the hang moves; the real J3 matcher is built in Part B.

Direction A, Nocturne: a dark, near-black room where the mood is a single glow of light. The field has no visible edge beyond a faint ring. Valence moves the glow from deep indigo through rose to warm amber and lightens it; arousal tightens the glow, saturates it and quickens its breathing, and a soft wash of the same color fills the screen. Type is one serif (Newsreader), set large and light for the room name, italic for the prompt and captions. An entry control, "Step in", fades up only after the first move, and the light opens to fill the screen as the Room appears. The Room is a museum hang on a charcoal wall: one work per row on a phone and three across on desktop, each at its own proportions, sitting on a shared hang line with a soft shadow. Captions and the Closer and Drift controls are quiet text buttons. The compact field is a small glowing disc in the header, and the describe box is an underlined line of serif text.

Direction B, Print Room: a daylit print shop on warm paper with black ink. The mood field is a visible disc whose surface is the color map itself: heavy slate blue on the left to bright gold on the right, washed toward paper when calm and heated toward red when charged. The point is a crisp ink ring with hairlines across the disc, and ripples spread from it faster as arousal rises. Type is one grotesk (Archivo) used condensed, bold and uppercase for the prompt and room name, and plain for everything else. The entry is a solid "Hang the room" button beside the prompt. The Room is a dense, numbered grid, two across on a phone and four on desktop. Every work sits contained (never cropped) in an identical square mat, so posters and fine art get the same cell. A sticky bar carries the small disc, the room name and a boxed describe field, and Closer and Drift are outlined buttons under each work. B departs on purpose from the product brief's "dark, quiet screen" to give a real contrast.

Scott's pick and why: neither. Both fields were abstract and description based (light, weather, color space) when the dial should be about how the visitor feels. Scott asked for a color wheel driven by emotions, with the feeling named as the point moves, and for the feelings to lead to the descriptions (D-044, D-045).

## Round 1: the feeling wheel
Date: 2026-10-14
What Scott asked for: an emotion-based dial ("how do you feel"), still color based, with the emotions named as the wheel moves, starting from the 12 emotion words at the positions measured from the catalog. In the Room, the feeling leads, then a sentence.
What changed: a new direction at /design-review/c. The Threshold wheel is painted from the 12 feelings: each word has its own color, placed at its catalog position (D-044), and the colors blend across the dark disc. No words sit on the wheel. As the point moves, the nearest feeling is named in large type below it, with "a little" and a second feeling when the point sits between two. The screen's wash and the point's glow take the current feeling's color, and the point breathes faster as arousal rises. The Gap G12 prompt stays for now (Q-009), and "Show me the room" appears after the first move. The Room leads with the feeling, followed by its draft sentence (12 drafts, for Scott's approval) and the bucket room name as a small label above (Q-010). The gallery is a dark grid, two across on a phone and three on desktop, with every work contained in the same mat and rounded Closer and Drift buttons. A and B stay in place, unchosen, until the design is settled.
Scott's pick and why: C is "much closer" to what he imagined. He kept the Gap G12 prompt (D-046) and the room name as a small label above the feeling (D-047), and asked to take the feeling wheel back into the A and B directions.

## Round 2: A and B on the feeling wheel
Date: 2026-10-14
What Scott asked for: rebuild A (Nocturne) and B (Print Room) from the updated spec: the feeling wheel (D-044), the feeling then a sentence in the Room (D-045), the prompt kept (D-046) and the room name as a label above the feeling (D-047).
What changed: the wheel, the feeling colors, the naming rule and the draft sentences moved into one shared module so A, B and C behave the same and differ only in look. A, Nocturne: the 12 feelings are soft blurred lights in a dark field with no hard edge, and the single glow of light takes the color of the feeling under it, tightening and quickening as arousal rises. The feeling is named below in large light serif, with "a little" and a second feeling in italic. "Step in" appears after the first move and the light opens into the Room. The Room keeps the museum hang at each work's own proportions, with quiet text buttons, and leads with the room name as a small caps label, the feeling, and the sentence in italic. B, Print Room: the same feeling colors printed on a paper disc with an ink rim, crosshairs and the ink ring point with ripples. The prompt sits above a rule, the feeling is stamped in condensed bold capitals with a bar of its color, and "Hang the room" is always available. The Room leads with the room name label, the feeling in condensed capitals, and the sentence beside a bar of the feeling's color, over the numbered square-mat grid with outlined Closer and Drift. C stays in place, unchanged in look, for comparison.
Scott's pick and why: Nocturne (A), as the direction to iterate from. He also changed how the wheel places the feelings (D-049).

## Round 3: Nocturne on an even wheel
Date: 2026-10-15
What Scott asked for: build D-049 on Nocturne. The 12 feelings sit evenly around the wheel, and the Room's mood comes from the feelings' catalog positions.
What changed: the feelings now sit 30 degrees apart at 0.75 of the radius, so the lights form an even ring of color around a dark, neutral center, and every part of the disc names a feeling. The name comes from the point's angle; "a little" and the second feeling appear when the point is at least a quarter of the way toward the next one. The Room's mood, its bucket room name and its hang come from the catalog positions of the two feelings either side, blended by angle and scaled by distance from center, so the rim at melancholy now opens "Overcast" with melancholy work. Closer and Drift move that mood and the point follows it on the wheel. B and C use the same wheel logic but are no longer being iterated.
Scott's pick and why: "Round 3 is good for now", then on 2026-10-15 "design is settled".
